Anatomy of a Cut

One expert operator’s procedure, decomposed. Three runs on video became 28 atomic actions, grouped into 10 tasks and 4 steps — and 13 points where she stops to make a judgment.

4 steps10 tasks 28 atomic actions13 judgment points

Widths are proportional to the number of actions each block contains, and gaps widen at task and step boundaries — so the spacing of the barcode is the structure of the work, not decoration.

Why decompose it this way

Steps are the phases a written procedure would name. Tasks group contiguous actions serving one purpose. Atomic actions are single acts, each two to five seconds of video.

The level a written SOP usually omits is the last one: the judgment points. Three of the highlighted actions are how this operator controls her own output — she aims the apple before cutting, keeps the cut visible while cutting, and checks the slice after. The fourth stage is not an action at all. It is a decision: a slice judged too thin is set aside rather than kept.
